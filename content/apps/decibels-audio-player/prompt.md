# Audio Player setup prompt

Set up GNOME Audio Player (app ID `org.gnome.Decibels`) from its official GNOME source and Flathub listing (https://apps.gnome.org/Decibels/). Inventory Linux architecture, Flatpak scopes, any existing ref, application-data ownership, remembered-file and playback-state counts, file-portal grants, codec runtime, audio-server type, available-sink count, mute state, sandbox overrides and backup coverage without printing filenames, paths, metadata, waveform contents or listening history. Install the stable Flathub ref in the existing ownership scope. Do not compile from source, launch the player, open or parse media, generate a waveform, play sound, seek, change speed, unmute audio or alter routing during setup.

Preserve remembered files and positions, playback speed, window state, portal grants, app and system volume, mute, output route, permissions, remotes, overrides and scope ownership. Local recordings may contain private meetings, voice notes or unreleased music; redact filenames and tags. Do not enumerate media folders, build a library or upload metadata. The app requires no account, microphone, media-library crawl or network access for local playback.

Treat every audio container, tag, cover image and duration as untrusted parser input. Before later opening, confirm the exact user-selected file, detected type, size, checksum, symlink resolution, read-only intent and decoder availability. Apply resource limits before waveform generation. Confirm speed and seek boundaries, and never infer that pitch handling or duration claims are exact without testing. Playing multiple files can sum loudness unexpectedly: identify each stream, start one at a time on an approved sink at low volume, and provide an immediate stop action.

Verify GNOME provenance, app ID, manifest source, commit, runtime, executable, bundled codecs, files, checksum and declared permissions offline. Confirm no file was opened, decoded or remembered; no waveform, audio stream, seek, speed, volume, route, network or setting changed. On failure, rollback only the new Audio Player ref in its original scope; preserve shared runtimes, codecs, recents, positions, audio configuration, remotes, overrides and backups.
